What this is

Every ranked TagPro match the ranked replay listing will return, catalogued and kept: 123,882 match ids across 75 weeks, Mar 2025 to Aug 2026, with the recording itself held for 10,503 of them and a tagpro.eu record linked for 121,609.

It exists because I like data and this data was not kept anywhere. Nobody had ranked matches in a form you could query, so I started keeping them.

Why the recordings stop at 8%

Because of the two-day wall. I was told not to scrape replays older than two days, and the site itself will not show a replay older than two days to anyone without an account. When I asked for a bulk download of the older material, I was declined.

So the archive is built inside those limits: a rolling window, taking each match while it is still young enough to be taken. That is why the two halves of this site look so different. The ids are complete, because the listing enumerates them and enumerating is cheap. The recordings are not, because every match older than the window when collection reached it was already out of reach, and still is.

The gap is historical and it does not close on its own. Every week that runs from here forward is collected in full. Nothing that aged out before then comes back by waiting.

If you have replays, I want them

This is the part where you can actually help. If you have a bulk replay dump — anything you exported, scraped, recorded, or have sitting in a folder from years ago — send it to metjr_ on Discord. Any format, any size, any date range, however messy.

Recordings already held here are matched by uuid, so overlap costs nothing and there is no need to check first or filter anything out. A dump of a thousand matches where nine hundred are already here is still a hundred matches this archive did not have. The missing list is published if you would rather target the holes, but please do not let that stop you sending the whole thing.

How the numbers are made

Two sources describe the same matches and neither is complete on its own.

  • The ranked replay listing is the authority on which matches exist. Its ids are what the coverage tables count, so those totals are exact rather than estimated.
  • tagpro.eu is the second source: the box score, the events, the players. About 98% of matches have one. The two are matched on start time, allowing 120 seconds of disagreement between them, because they record it in different time zones and to different precision.
  • 292 tagpro.eu records were rebuilt here out of archived recordings, for matches the mirror never carried. That is the orange band on the coverage table — progress against a shortfall, not a share of the whole.
  • 8 matches are exceptions: they appear on tagpro.eu but the ranked listing returns nothing for them, including inside date ranges it reported as fully enumerated. There is no recording to collect for those.

Two ids identify a match. uuid identifies the replay; game_id addresses the recording itself and is what a recording is requested by; eu_match_id is the same match on tagpro.eu. The full field reference is in DATA_MAP.md.

How it runs

A pipeline on a home machine follows the listing continuously, pulls each replay while it is still reachable, links it to its tagpro.eu record, and rebuilds records for matches the mirror missed. This site is regenerated from that database and republished on a schedule, so the tables are never far behind. The 3.9 GB of recordings live on that machine and are served from it directly — see the Download tab.

Numbers on this page come from the same database as the coverage tables and move with them. Field reference: DATA_MAP.md.
