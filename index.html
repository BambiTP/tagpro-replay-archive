We know what happened in almost every ranked match. We have the recording for one in sixteen.

Ranked replays stop being reachable a couple of days after a match ends. Everything older than that survives only if somebody saved it. This is a running count of what has been saved, what has not, and what is still recoverable.

Each ids link downloads that week’s full match list as gzipped CSV — every match UUID and game id, with flags for whether the replay and the match record are held. The complete inventory is 120,451 rows across 75 files, about 6 MB.

Which weeks are short

StatusWeeksMeaning
Complete2every known match from that week has its replay
Partial6some replays held, some missing
Nothing held67not a single replay from that week survives here

67 of 75 weeks have no replays at all. Everything from March 2025 through July 2026 is almost entirely unrecorded. Those matches are past the point where they can be collected by anyone outside the people who run the servers.

Match records are nearly complete

70 weeks are missing at least one match record, but the shortfall is small — 2,255 matches in total, 1.9% of everything. The scan that discovers which ranked matches exist covers the full history from 24 March 2025 to now with no gaps, so the list of what is missing is believed complete rather than merely what has been noticed.

How this can be helped

Play and keep your own matches

The single thing that needs no permission from anyone: save the replays of matches you play in. That is your own game data, downloaded the ordinary way the game already offers. Enough people doing that and the archive stops losing ground from today onward, regardless of what happens with the backlog.

Donate an archive you already have

People have been saving replays privately for years. If you have a folder of them, it is likely to contain matches nothing else does — a recent donation of 2,517 files added 2,515 matches from November 2025 that were not held anywhere in this archive. Old and partial collections are still worth having.

What is deliberately not here

There is no scraping tool, endpoint list, or collection script on this page, and there will not be. The people who run the ranked servers asked for the crawling to stop, and it stopped. They have since allowed this archive to keep what it already had, and allowed recent matches to be collected inside a narrow window. That arrangement is worth more than anything a crawl would retrieve, and it is not transferable — it was extended to one person, not handed out.

The backlog is not something a crowd can collect. It exists in one place, and it is released or it is not.

The ask

The 112,885 missing recordings are already itemised — every id, every date, published above and downloadable in full. If bulk access is ever granted, the list of exactly what to send is ready and costs nobody any work to produce.

Finding a specific match

The ranked replay browser on the official site is backed by a JSON endpoint that takes the same filters its own controls set. Reading it directly is usually the quickest way to locate one particular game — a match you played, a notable final, a game on a specific map.

https://tagpro.koalabeast.com/replays/data?page=0&pageSize=50&visibility=ranked&dateStart=<ms>&dateEnd=<ms>

The timestamp format

Both date filters are Unix time in milliseconds — thirteen digits, which is ordinary Unix seconds multiplied by 1000. A few ways to produce one:

  • Shell: date -u -d "2026-08-01" +%s000
  • Browser console: Date.parse("2026-08-01T00:00:00Z")
  • Python: int(datetime(2026,8,1,tzinfo=timezone.utc).timestamp()*1000)

A 13-digit number starting with 17 is somewhere in the 2023–2026 range; if yours has 10 digits you have seconds and need three more zeroes.

Downloading one recording

Once you have a game id, the recording itself comes from:

https://tagpro.koalabeast.com/replays/gameFile?gameId=<game_id>&gameUser=<user>

It returns the entire frame-by-frame recording as newline-delimited JSON, and the files are large — a couple of megabytes each is normal. The first line on its own carries the match metadata, including the ranked skill ratings and win probabilities, if that is all you need.

Recordings older than roughly two days return 403 you must be logged in to view that replay to anonymous requests. That is why this archive holds almost nothing from before mid-2026, and why the 112,765 missing recordings listed here are not something a crowd can go and collect. They survive in one place, and they are released or they are not.

The 250-result ceiling

page stops at 4 and pageSize stops at 50, so any single query returns at most 250 matches, whatever the filters. There is no way to page deeper. If the window you asked for holds more than 250 games, you are seeing a slice of it and the rest is not reachable by adding pages — narrow the date range until the result set fits underneath the cap.

This is also why the filters are for finding games rather than collecting them in bulk. Please keep requests to the pace of a person looking something up. These are volunteer-run servers, and heavy automated traffic against them has caused real outages before — including from this project, which is why the crawling stopped.

Where the data lives

Four separate things get called “replay data” and they are not interchangeable. What is scarce is only the last one.

LayerWhat it isCoverage
Match listwhich ranked matches exist at all — ids, dates, maps, scorescomplete for the whole ranked era
Match recordsplayers, scores, event timelines, per-match stats, mirrored on tagpro.eu98.1% of known matches
Ranked metadataskill ratings and win probabilities attached to a ranked gameonly where the recording was captured
Replay recordingsthe full game file everything else is derived from6.2% — the gap this page is about

The first two are broadly available and always have been. The recordings are the part that expires: reachable for a couple of days after a match, then effectively gone unless somebody saved a copy. That is the whole reason this archive exists, and the reason its coverage before mid-2026 is close to zero.

Per-week inventories of every known match id are downloadable from the table above. The full list of what is missing is published as one file too: missing_replays.csv.gz — 112,765 rows, 4.8 MB. The data map describes every field.

Honesty about how this got here

This archive started by crawling the ranked servers without asking. The operators noticed, it cost them bandwidth and time, and they asked for it to stop. It stopped. They were within their rights to require everything collected be deleted and did not. Anyone weighing whether to trust this project should know that, and it is written here rather than left to be discovered.
